# 2. STORY, PLAYER AGENCY AND FIVE-MINUTE TIMING

## 2.1 The hard timing target

The title menu and settings are **outside the clock**. Start the demonstration clock on PLAY; pause/alt-tab time does not count. A successful first-time player should complete either path in **approximately 280–300 seconds** (target 4:40 with 20 seconds of human variability), without forced teleportation, forced quest auto-completion or rushing dialogue. A slower player can finish naturally after five minutes; the goal is a reliable **presentation route**, not a hard fail timer.

| Wall-clock after PLAY | Duration | Required beat | Player control |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 00:00–00:25 | 25s | Comet resumes, fracture, descent, impact, Star awakening | Brief cinematic → interactive awakening |
| 00:25–00:55 | 30s | Follow wisp, discover Child, one expressive interaction, form bond | Fully playable with short contextual camera moment |
| 00:55–01:30 | 35s | Approach cottage, witness 2–3 fast visual memories, choose Tether or Roots | Movement → interactive choice; choice never auto-selects |
| 01:30–02:10 | 40s | Branch Task 1 | Gameplay |
| 02:10–02:55 | 45s | Branch Task 2 | Gameplay |
| 02:55–03:45 | 50s | Branch Task 3 | Gameplay |
| 03:45–04:25 | 40s | Distinct final sequence | Short player action + cinematic resolution |
| 04:25–04:40 | 15s | Epilogue and “Experience the other wish” | UI |

**Build an authored compact critical-path play space.** Existing island size is about 920 m; do not make players cross the entire island. Critical-path walking distances should fit the time budget at the actual Star movement speed, with <10–12 seconds between major landmarks. Use naturally connected terraces, switchbacks, bridges and carefully staged camera transitions—not arbitrary objective teleports. If a major physical distance must be bridged, design a purposeful 2–4-second interactive light-flight, guided lift or authored traversal shot that preserves spatial continuity.

### Scene direction / pacing rules

- Every 5–10 seconds the player sees something **new**: character action, environmental transformation, mechanical twist, striking composition or emotional revelation.
- No unskippable dialogue line longer than necessary. Use concise on-screen lines, meaningful gestures and music instead of long paragraphs.
- Player is never stuck waiting for a villager to complete a long idle animation. NPC performance and interaction are synchronized via events.
- During cinematics, camera framing keeps the Star, Child and narrative object visible, with screen-space safe areas and no occlusion by trees/roofs.
- Choice is not a morality test. Tether = legitimate homecoming with painful trade-offs; Roots = legitimate belonging with sacrificed celestial certainty. Both must be equally beautiful and valid.
- The first 90 seconds must clearly establish **Star needs fragments**, **fragments sustain village**, **Child needs companionship**, and **choice changes the world**.
- Preserve optional village life as atmosphere, but do not require lengthy pebble stacking, three errands, or repeated identical button holds on the critical path.

## 2.2 Shared opening: exact beats

**0–6s — Celestial breath.** Title already showed the suspended comet. PLAY releases time with smooth acceleration. A close, crisp silhouette of the comet's nucleus reveals layered rotating facets, elegant tail ribbons and delicate star-dust. Avoid overexposed white blob.

**6–12s — Fracture.** A visible crack travels through the nucleus, three luminous fragments peel away on physically legible trajectories, and sound briefly drops out. Show where fragments are going: a cottage window, a well/garden, the mountain. This is the story setup, not random fireworks.

**12–20s — Impact.** The comet passes through atmosphere: air compression, hot-to-cool chromatic transition, displaced cloud wisps, ribbon turbulence, then beach contact. A single tasteful impact shockwave, localized sand plume, and crater reveal. No prolonged screen shake; camera quickly settles.

**20–25s — Awakening.** Ground-level shot. Dust falls, the Star's body pulses, the player receives movement control, and the Guide Wisp appears from one lingering fragment. The Child's distant silhouette is readable behind a tree. Transition into gameplay with no black-frame hitch.

**25–55s — Child encounter.** The wisp leads toward a small clearing. Child peeks, approaches, and asks one short question. The player presses/holds F to shine; the Child visibly reacts, offers a hand, and follows. If player does nothing for ~5 seconds, gentle wisp/body language prompts without taking away agency. Compress `ChildConversation.cs` substantially while preserving its best emotional moments.

**55–90s — Cottage and the choice.** Guide Wisp threads through a lively village. At the cottage, show **three short memory tableaux**: warmth at Grandmother's hearth, well water returning, seeds awakening. Each 3–4 seconds; crossfade through shared visual motifs rather than separate long sequences. Child says, in essence, “Your light helped us. But it's yours.” The player chooses **Tether (hold F)** or **Roots (press E)**. No escape/skip can silently choose. Confirm selection through an unmistakable visual action and begin the corresponding path immediately.

## 2.3 Single authoritative choice architecture

The current project has a real integration problem: `CottageChoiceScene.Update()` launches `FragmentBlessing.Run()`, and `FragmentBlessing.Conclude()` calls `host.CompleteChoice()` directly. This can bypass `CottageChoiceScene.Tether()` / `.Roots()` consequences. **Fix it, do not work around it with extra flags.**

Create a **single authoritative `WishboundStoryDirector`** (or refactor `CottageChoiceScene` into one) that owns: `Intro → ChildMet → CottageMemory → ChoicePending → TetherTasks | RootsTasks → Finale → Epilogue`. It alone commits alignment, invokes route transition, updates Child disposition, and signals wisp/music/camera/world reactions. `FragmentBlessing` is presentation only and reports the player's selection without writing branch flags or starting quests independently. Exactly-once choice commit, with duplicate event protection.

```csharp
// Architectural intent; integrate with actual StoryFlags, save APIs and scene references.
public enum WishboundRoute { None, Tether, Roots }
public enum DemoPhase { Opening, ChildMeeting, CottageMemory, Choice, Route, Finale, Epilogue }

public sealed class WishboundStoryDirector : MonoBehaviour
{
    public static WishboundStoryDirector Instance { get; private set; }
    public DemoPhase Phase { get; private set; }
    public WishboundRoute Route { get; private set; }
    public event System.Action<WishboundRoute> RouteCommitted;
    bool choiceCommitted;

    public bool TryCommitChoice(WishboundRoute choice)
    {
        if (Phase != DemoPhase.Choice || choiceCommitted || choice == WishboundRoute.None)
            return false;
        choiceCommitted = true;
        Route = choice;
        StoryFlags.Alignment = choice == WishboundRoute.Tether
            ? StarAlignment.Tether : StarAlignment.Roots;
        StoryFlags.Set("CottageChoiceMade", true);
        // Emit exactly one authoritative event. A dedicated presenter animates
        // glass fracture vs handprint, Child disposition, music and environment.
        RouteCommitted?.Invoke(choice);
        Phase = DemoPhase.Route;
        return true;
    }
}
```

The code above is **illustrative**, not guaranteed drop-in. Use explicit serialized references, avoid ambiguous `FindFirstObjectByType` dependencies, and ensure migration from existing alignment/spectrum values is correct. Define a `NewDemoRun()` that clears **all** demo progression state (including ending flags and child-hand state), not only a handful of PlayerPrefs keys. Existing saved free-roam progress must not contaminate a presentation run.

### Story reactions that must be consistent

- **Tether choice:** cottage glass cracks with an authored 2D/3D shatter, the fragment returns to the Star, interior warmth falls briefly, Child shields eyes and lets go. Later, after the Star helps villagers and the Child at the ferry/garden, they reconcile naturally: the Child may offer a hand again at the summit. **Do not call `TakeHand()` immediately after the Tether rejection** as current `FragmentBlessing.Finish()` effectively does.
- **Roots choice:** a luminous handprint blooms on the glass, warmth remains inside, Star radiance softens and becomes more tactile, Child takes the Star's hand. Villagers' lanterns respond gently, not with a blinding explosion.
- These choices change **shader palette, particles, music stems, idle animation, child following distance, villager reactions, quest VFX and ending**. They must not merely change text labels.

# 3. TWO FULLY PLAYABLE BRANCHES — MECHANICS, SETPIECES AND ACCEPTANCE

The six main tasks are not six generic “walk up and press E” events. Each must have **anticipation → player decision/skill → response → character reaction → environmental consequence → clear completion**. Maintain a **single coherent mechanic vocabulary**:

- **Movement:** current WASD / gamepad left stick.
- **Interact / Reach:** E / appropriate gamepad button.
- **Starlight Pulse:** hold F / right trigger; pulse is an intentional charge with feedback, not a universal solution.
- **Dim Light:** hold C / mapped gamepad control; enables shadow-world perception.
- **Jump / Ground-slam:** existing jump, then hold C while airborne (if supported; otherwise introduce a clearly taught contextual action).
- **Companion action:** contextual E near a companion affordance; no additional confusing keybind.
- Prompts should be small, elegant, contextual and accessible; controls must be remappable or at least visibly documented.

## 3A. TETHER — “THE WAY BACK TO THE STARS”

### T1. Dusk-Ferry Passage (40s)

**Space:** a 22–30 m river crossing with a weathered timber ferry pier, mooring ropes, moss-covered stone abutments, reed beds, rippling water, cargo crates and lanterns. The ferry keeper is a real animated settler, not a text popup. The Child hesitates at the water.

**Action sequence:**
1. The Guide Wisp circles a distinctive cloth-wrapped parcel. Ferry keeper looks up from tying a rope and gestures toward it; the player collects it via a **0.5–0.8s telekinetic lift** with soft contact glow and hand/Star animation.
2. Six **authored stepping stones** are partly submerged. Their glyphs appear only while the Star dims. Player holds C, sees the path emerge, and crosses 3–4 stable traversal points while the Child follows on a synchronized safe route. Remaining stones can be decorative/optional; do not demand six slow individual interactions.
3. The Child nearly hesitates; the player briefly illuminates a safe landing without fully blinding the shadow path. This is the meaningful interplay: dim to perceive, pulse to reassure. Allow a simple forgiving version for accessibility.
4. Parcel is placed into a keeper's satchel/receiving rack. Keeper physically takes it, nods, and lights a guiding lantern. A bridge/path ahead opens visually; wisp transfers to the garden.

**Failure and recovery:** fall → immediate nearby bank/stone checkpoint with a 0.4s water-splash dissolve, parcel retained; Child never teleports visibly in front of the camera. If the player waits >6s, wisp demonstrates the dim action once. No permanent failure, no physics pileups.

**Assets:** hero pier, ferry hull or moored skiff, ropes, parcel, stone glyph set, water decals, reeds, keeper clothing and satchel, lantern, 2D splash/foam VFX. See asset-construction chapters.

**Completion gate:** parcel handed over AND crossing reached. One event `Tether.FerryComplete`, fired once; scene visibly responds.

### T2. Frost-Locked Seedbed (45s)

**Space:** a terraced hillside garden with cracked slate planters, frost-coated climbing vines, three carved prism housings, a small spring and a gardener settler. This should be a striking visual contrast: desaturated icy foreground transforming into warm greens and luminous flowers.

**Action sequence:**
1. Gardener points to the dormant seedbed. Player sees three prisms; wisp briefly sketches a ribbon of light connecting them.
2. Player rotates **two meaningful prisms** (third is a fixed receiver) by E or held directional input. A real reflected beam changes direction continuously and snaps with a satisfying resonant chime when aligned. Use authored crystal geometry with internal facet masks, not cylinders.
3. The beam weakens frost; player channels F to thaw one tangled vine gate. Gardener moves in **proactively** to water exposed soil with a proper watering animation and liquid arc. The Child carries a small pail and tips it together with the gardener; player does **not** run a separate fetch errand.
4. A timed growth sequence plays: soil cracks, stems unfold with spline/bone deformation, leaves turn toward light, flowers open in staggered waves, and insects return. One fragment resonates; the Star gains a visible new flight ribbon.

**Failure and recovery:** prism angle snap tolerances generous (e.g., ±10–12°); no pixel hunting. If beam is blocked, visual feedback explains why. No task completion until the garden visibly blooms.

**Completion gate:** beam aligned, thaw action performed, growth animation finished, `Tether.GardenComplete` event emitted.

### T3. The Last Light (50s)

**Space:** a short 25–35 m summit ascent through a cliffside arch, suspended root bridges, layered clouds, ruined star-carved stairs and a high overlook. The last fragment hangs inside an ornate ring of celestial stone; the Child is visible at the lookout bench.

**Action sequence:**
1. Guide Wisp demonstrates the Star's **newly empowered float** across a short gap. Player uses an already supported float/jump mechanic; adjust controller parameters based on Tether alignment and show readable wind ribbons. The gap is forgiving and has a recovery ledge.
2. The Star passes a pair of old stone arches with **light-activated engravings**. Rather than a second generic puzzle, the player channels F while moving to illuminate the final ascent; the environment responds continuously.
3. At the last fragment, the Child asks one concise emotional question. Player **holds E to reclaim** it while a visible tether draws through the Star. The Child steps back, then approaches with a genuine goodbye. Camera eases into the ending only after the player confirms.

**Completion gate:** fragment reclaimed and farewell initiated; `Tether.FinalFragmentComplete` once.

### Tether finale (40s)

- 0–8s: Star rises slowly from the lookout, Child reaches toward it, a warm light briefly connects them.
- 8–20s: fragments form a beautiful articulated constellation around the Star; the camera reveals the village below, still glowing from what the Star helped restore. No instant sky teleport.
- 20–32s: Star climbs through painted cloud layers, becomes a point in a constellation; the Child's silhouette remains readable against the skyline.
- 32–40s: final quiet shot of the Child looking upward, one last warm sparkle falling near their hand, musical cadence. Fade to epilogue.
- Use an **authored animation/Timeline or deterministic cinematic director**, depth-composed shots, layered sky materials, custom ribbons, timed SFX and music stems. Avoid a 6-second vertical translation plus `Teleport()` as the entire ending.

## 3B. ROOTS — “A PLACE TO BELONG”

### R1. Hearth of Many Hands (40s)

**Space:** intimate village square with a real masonry hearth, stacked firewood, woven blankets, uneven stone path, herb beds, carved benches, market baskets and multiple purposeful villagers. The hearth fire must be **stylized 2D animated flames**, not blurry particles.

**Action sequence:**
1. Guide Wisp settles beside a patch of cold soil. Child kneels and places a seed; player channels F gently to warm the earth while keeping the Star's light **small and controlled**.
2. An elder approaches and offers a copper watering vessel; the gardener brings compost or mulch. Their gestures and prop handoffs are synchronized. Player presses E once to complete the shared planting; soil visibly changes, a plant unfurls, and the hearth flame steadies.
3. The Child invites the Star to watch villagers gathering around the renewed hearth. A 3–4-second in-engine character beat plays **without a full camera takeover**. One character waves, another brings food, another sits. This is a social reward, not an arbitrary quest checklist.
4. Pebble stacking/stone skipping remain **optional polished 5–10s interactions** outside the mandatory path; the main task stays on schedule.

**Completion gate:** planting and communal hearth response, `Roots.HearthComplete` once. Village ambience and local light visibly warm.

### R2. Whisper Canyon (45s)

**Space:** narrow natural canyon with sculpted strata, hanging roots, damp stone, moss, carved guardian motifs and a concealed starfall mural. Light shafts and an authored 2D flame/ember shrine accent the environment. The path should be mysterious but not dark enough to frustrate play.

**Action sequence:**
1. Guide Wisp performs a curious orbit at a sealed stone disc, then hides behind it to draw attention to an engraved indentation. Child points at the seal.
2. Player jumps and **ground-slams** with C. The seal cracks in an authored radial pattern; displaced stone wedges animate and settle with dust cards, not an exploding primitive sphere.
3. Inside, player channels F along the mural. Illumination follows the Star's position, revealing painted layers progressively: falling star, lonely Child, wish at the observatory. The Child's expression changes from wonder to vulnerability.
4. The wisp becomes quiet; a single short line establishes the emotional reveal. Guide Wisp points toward the observatory, not through a wall.

**Completion gate:** seal broken AND mural revealed, `Roots.MuralComplete` once. Keep narrative reveal readable for a first-time viewer in 6–8 seconds.

### R3. Observatory Wish (50s)

**Space:** a hand-crafted ruined observatory perched over the valley: engraved circular floor, broken brass astrolabe, brass-and-stone armillary ring, star charts, cracked lens, dangling cloth, wild vines, a luminous beam and a distant view of the village. Every component must be a proper detailed mesh/material, not a torus and box assembly with emissive color.

**Action sequence:**
1. The Child arrives slightly ahead and looks at a star map. The player approaches the central lens; a subtle interactive star-map projection reveals the Child's original wish: **someone who would stay**.
2. The celestial beam grows brighter, offering a path home. Instead of a text-menu second choice, the player physically **walks away from the beam** toward the Child; camera and music acknowledge the movement. Do not make the player guess: Child, wisp and composition guide them.

# 4. ART DIRECTION AND THE “NO GEOMETRIC PLACEHOLDERS” CONTRACT

## 4.1 The visual identity

**Wishbound's world must look hand-crafted, warm, tactile, and emotionally directed.** Use a consistent language of asymmetrical forms, gently exaggerated proportions, bevels, soft silhouettes, painted color variation, and purposeful negative space. Stylized does **not** mean low effort, flat boxes, neon emissive spheres or generic low-poly facets.

- **Shape language:** curved roof ridges, tapered timber, rounded worn stone edges, slightly crooked chimneys, deep window reveals, layered foliage masses, irregular but structurally believable stairs. Avoid perfectly straight repeated boxes and copy-pasted houses.
- **Material language:** painted stone with warm/cool mineral variation, wood with restrained visible grain and edge wear, roof tile with irregular terracotta patches, linen with weave, brass with oxidized patina, glass with thickness and hand-painted highlights, moss in crevices. Textures should be painterly rather than photoreal PBR noise.
- **Color story:** opening = deep indigo, cobalt, magenta-gold; village = sage, honey, terracotta, teal; Tether = moonlit cyan, violet, amber accents; Roots = warm cream, fern green, hearth gold and soft coral. Preserve natural contrast and character readability.
- **Lighting:** one coherent sun/moon key direction, gentle fill and bounce, visible contact shadows, physically motivated local lights. Light must sculpt form; bloom must **not** erase the geometry.
- **Animation:** even static spaces breathe: cloth sways, grass bends, lanterns move, leaves respond to wind, hearth flickers, water curls around stones, flowers turn toward light. Motion must be low-amplitude and purposeful.
- **Composition:** hero landmarks have strong silhouettes from the actual player camera; avoid foliage obscuring the objective. Use foreground/midground/background layering, depth haze and color contrast.
- **Character scale:** Child roughly 1.2–1.3 m; adult villagers ~1.55–1.85 m; world props sized accordingly. Verify with existing controller/camera rather than assuming.

### Required look-development comparisons

Create at least six **consistent camera-angle look-dev captures**: opening comet, village hearth, ferry, frost garden, whisper canyon, observatory. Include a before/after for representative current geometry and final authored replacement. If image capture tools are unavailable, create a camera bookmark scene and report `NOT CAPTURED`; never claim an unseen visual result.

## 4.2 Actual asset-production pipeline (mandatory)

**Preferred toolchain:** Blender (installed locally or via command line) for mesh generation and authored modifiers; Python `bpy` for repeatable builds; image editing/Python for original painterly textures and 2D sprite sheets; Unity import/Editor scripts for prefab assembly, animation, colliders, LOD and lighting. ProBuilder can assist blockout only. If Blender is unavailable in the execution environment, create **equivalent detailed meshes using a Unity Editor `Mesh` builder** with real silhouette, UVs, tangents/normals, secondary details, baked `.asset` meshes and prefabs. Do not fall back to a few primitives.

**For every final hero asset:**

1. Write a short design sheet: name, role, dimensions, silhouette, material palette, intended interaction, animation, LOD and collision.
2. Build a **primary silhouette mesh** from curves/extrusions/lathed profiles/hand-shaped mesh loops. Introduce intentional asymmetry and slight curvature; bevel all hard edges appropriately. Keep physically believable joints and thickness.
3. Add **secondary forms**: joinery, grooves, trims, hinges, handles, rope knots, cracks, chips, engraved runes, weathering and small attachment points. These must read at gameplay distance; do not create invisible detail for its own sake.
4. Create a **UV layout** with controlled texel density and one or two shared trim/paint atlases where appropriate. Bake normal/AO/curvature masks if useful. No stretched UVs.
5. Author **original painterly albedo** with broad value variation and selective brush marks; add small-scale roughness variation and edge tint. Avoid random high-frequency noise that becomes visual mud.
6. Add collision as a **separate simple proxy** (capsules/boxes/convex meshes) that matches gameplay and does not snag the Star. The visible mesh is detailed; the collision need not be.
7. Make LOD0/LOD1/LOD2 (hero assets LOD1 ~50–60% triangles; LOD2 ~15–25%); author impostors or cull for small props where appropriate. Use smooth transitions or cross-fade to avoid popping.
8. Export with consistent axis and units, preserve scale and pivot, and import into Unity with correct normals/tangents, materials, and rig. Check flipped faces and transparency.
9. Assemble a **prefab** with meshes, colliders, material instances, animation/interaction sockets, event hooks, audio emitters and editor gizmos. Give each meaningful transform names and an asset manifest entry.
10. Place it in a playable scene, inspect from gameplay and cinematic cameras, profile it, and fix defects before marking complete.

**Art budget guidance (initial, profile and adjust):** hero building 8k–25k LOD0 triangles including modular detail; observatory 15k–35k; bridge/pier 5k–12k; hero props 1k–5k; background props 150–2k; main character 8k–20k; background villager 4k–12k; foliage instanced and LODed. These are **starting budgets, not hard universal limits**. Minimize material count and unnecessary overdraw; use shared atlases, GPU instancing, SRP Batcher and texture streaming.

### 4.3.1 Weathered cottage: exact modeling recipe

- Start with an **irregular 6.2 m × 4.8 m footprint** and ~4.2 m ridge height. Walls 0.35–0.45 m thick with slight lean, nonuniform plaster surfaces and exposed stone plinth.
- Roof: build two slightly curved slopes, with **real overlapping terracotta tiles** using instanced row meshes. Tile profile should have an arched cross-section and chipped edge; vary hue by seeded palette ±8–12%. Add ridge caps, end tiles, fascia, small moss clumps and gutter spouts. Do not put a flat orange plane on top.
- Timber: bevel 3–6 cm, use tapered ends and visible mortise/tenon details; add subtle wood grain normal/paint texture.
- Window: 1.2 × 1.0 m deep-set frame, divided panes, 2–3 mm glass thickness, slight tint, curtain fabric with pleats. The warm interior should contain visible furniture silhouettes so the window tells a story.
- **Tether**: model glass as pre-cut 8–14 irregular pieces or use a prepared fracture mesh; animate pieces along deterministic arcs and settle/fade; no runtime explosive mesh fracture required. Interior light shifts from warm to cool over ~1 second. Do not create an opaque flash hiding the consequence.
- **Roots**: use a dedicated decal mesh / shader mask of a handprint (existing `Assets/Art/Look/Handprint.png` can be a starting source), reveal with directional warmth and refracted edge; keep window intact.
- Add foliage that **avoids camera and interactable**, soft smoke from chimney and warm light bounce on surrounding stone.

### 4.3.2 Ferry and stone crossing: exact modeling recipe

- Pier: 2.2 m wide, 7–9 m long, planks 0.16–0.28 m wide, each with curved long edges, small splits, nail pegs and uneven end lengths. Supporting beams and vertical posts must make structural sense. Add rope from spline curves with braided texture or actual low-poly braid near camera.
- Skiff: 3.5–4.5 m long hull with 8–12 cross-section loops, tapered bow/stern, interior ribs, bench, oarlocks, wetline tint. A flat rectangular boat is unacceptable.
- Stones: each has distinct sculpted silhouette, 0.8–1.5 m width, 0.2–0.6 m visible height, slightly tilted top surface. Use layered beveled contour loops, chipped rock protrusions and carved glyph meshes/decals. Ensure 1.0 m or greater reliable landing surfaces for the controller.
- River: existing water shader may be reused but needs shoreline foam around stones, directional current, subtle refraction or depth color, and ripple decals at foot contact.

### 4.3.3 Frost garden: exact modeling recipe

- Garden occupies ~16 × 12 m with 2–3 curved terraces and a central axis of light. Distinct dark earth, slate, moss, snow/frost and warm foliage material zones.
- Prisms: faceted crystal with **beveled polygonal profile**, interior layered crystal, colored absorption, engraved base ring, 2–3 mechanical stops and a real pivot; transparent materials must avoid sorting artifacts.
- Vines: use **Bezier/spline-generated tapered tubes** and separately modeled leaf meshes. Store a reveal/melt value per vine via MaterialPropertyBlock or GPU instance data. Vines curl and relax as the frost retreats.
- Plant growth: author seedling stages (seed → stem → leaves → flower) with skinned mesh/bones, blend shapes or procedural spline growth, not scaling an entire mesh uniformly from zero. Stagger timing and vary species. Add subtle pollen motes only as an accent.
- Gardeners must visibly touch tools/soil at the correct animation event. Water should follow a narrow curved particle/sprite stream and visibly darken soil.

### 4.3.4 Canyon and mural: exact modeling recipe

- Build canyon walls from **interlocking stratified rock modules** with sculpted layered contours, not stacked cubes. Use a height/slope aware placement tool; repeat 3–5 variants with rotation and nonuniform scale, and add a few hero rocks with unique silhouettes.
- Seal: 2.5–3 m diameter, 0.35 m thick, concentric carved bands and central glyph, 8–12 prepared wedge sections with internal fracture faces. The slam causes a central crack, secondary cracks, then wedges fall and settle. Keep a safe navigable opening and update colliders once.
- Mural: original painted art on stone using a **layered texture atlas** (linework, pigments, emissive ink and rough stone mask). Use several UV-aligned panels or a single high-quality 2–4K texture; reveal by a shader mask traveling along engraved linework. The story imagery must be legible without subtitles.
- Use localized 2D torch flames and smoke sprites, moss and dripping water, but avoid heavy full-screen fog that hides the artwork.

### 4.3.5 Observatory: exact modeling recipe

- Main deck: ~14 m diameter stepped circular stone floor with hand-carved concentric rings, 3–5 broken wall arcs and a dramatic valley opening. Create a strong silhouette visible from the route entrance.
- Armillary: several **thin brass rings with actual thickness and bevel**, not simple untextured torus primitives. Add engraved tick marks, pivot mounts, star-map plates, chipped gilding and mechanical stops. Animate rings at different angular velocities, decelerating naturally at the Roots finale.
- Lens: multi-layer curved glass, brass rim, inset clamps and scratches; separate beam mesh and 2D projected rune patterns. Beam should cast colored pools onto nearby surfaces without overpowering exposure.

## 4.4 Blender scripting reference: authored stone module

The following **example** demonstrates the expected *level of construction* for one hero stone, including a non-cubic silhouette, surface contour, bevel, secondary carved inset and a material. It is a starting point, not a substitute for detailed final art. Extend it to create three distinct stone silhouettes, proper UVs, atlas masks, LODs and exported assets. Run with Blender Python (`blender -b --python ...`) after adapting the output path and verifying the installed Blender version.

```python
# Example: Assets/WishboundVerticalSlice/Art/Source/build_hero_stones.py
# Requires Blender's bpy. Deterministic, real mesh with multi-ring silhouette.
import bpy, math, random
from mathutils import Vector

rng = random.Random(147)
bpy.ops.object.select_all(action='SELECT')
bpy.ops.object.delete(use_global=False)

def paint_material(name, rgb, roughness=0.92):
    m = bpy.data.materials.new(name)
    m.diffuse_color = (*rgb, 1)
    m.use_nodes = True
    bsdf = m.node_tree.nodes.get('Principled BSDF')
    bsdf.inputs['Base Color'].default_value = (*rgb, 1)
    bsdf.inputs['Roughness'].default_value = roughness
    return m

stone = paint_material('WB_Stone_WarmSlate', (.31, .36, .37))
carve = paint_material('WB_Stone_Glyph', (.16, .27, .29))

# Irregular layered contour: bottom narrower, shoulder wide, top worn.
N = 18
rings = [(0.00, .72), (.11, .90), (.27, 1.00), (.43, .95), (.49, .82)]
angles = [2 * math.pi * i / N for i in range(N)]
radial = [rng.uniform(.91, 1.09) for _ in range(N)]
verts = []
for z, width in rings:
    for i, a in enumerate(angles):
        r = radial[i] * width
        verts.append((math.cos(a) * r * .78, math.sin(a) * r * .61, z + rng.uniform(-.008, .008)))
faces = []
for j in range(len(rings)-1):
    for i in range(N):
        k = j*N+i; nxt = j*N+(i+1)%N
        faces.append((k, nxt, nxt+N, k+N))
faces.append(tuple(reversed(tuple(range(N)))))
faces.append(tuple((len(rings)-1)*N+i for i in range(N)))
mesh = bpy.data.meshes.new('Stone_AuthoredMesh')
mesh.from_pydata(verts, [], faces); mesh.update()
obj = bpy.data.objects.new('WB_ShadowStone_A', mesh)
bpy.context.collection.objects.link(obj)
obj.data.materials.append(stone)
for p in mesh.polygons: p.use_smooth = True

# Bevel/weighted normals; preserve silhouette while softening highlights.
bevel = obj.modifiers.new('HandWornEdges', 'BEVEL')
bevel.width = .045; bevel.segments = 2
normal = obj.modifiers.new('WeightedNormals', 'WEIGHTED_NORMAL')

# A real recessed rune can be modeled as an inset curve or baked from a
# high-resolution source. This example creates a shallow inset spiral.
curve = bpy.data.curves.new('WB_StoneRune_Curve', 'CURVE')
curve.dimensions = '3D'; curve.bevel_depth = .008; curve.bevel_resolution = 3
spline = curve.splines.new('POLY'); spline.points.add(35)
for i, point in enumerate(spline.points):
    t = i/35
    a = t * math.pi * 3.2
    r = .28 * (1-t)
    point.co = (math.cos(a)*r, math.sin(a)*r, .505, 1)
rune = bpy.data.objects.new('WB_StoneRune', curve)
bpy.context.collection.objects.link(rune); rune.data.materials.append(carve)

# Follow with: UV unwrap, painted texture bake, collider, LOD variants,
# deterministic export to Assets/WishboundVerticalSlice/Art/Models/Quest/.
# Export format/axis must be verified against Unity import settings.
```

## 5.2 Core toon lighting behavior

- Three broad diffuse regions: **lit**, **mid**, **shadow**, plus narrow controlled transition bands to prevent harsh cheap posterization.
- Shadows are **cool and colored**, never dead gray; sunlight is slightly warm; interior hearth light should shift nearby colors plausibly.
- Painted albedo carries most texture identity. Normal map adds small tactile detail, not photorealistic bumps.
- Use AO only in contact and crevices. Avoid dirty global darkening.
- Rim light is scene-aware, low intensity, and especially useful for the Child, Star and important silhouettes.
- Stylized specular is reserved for brass, wet stone, crystal, eyes and water; matte plaster should not look plastic.
- Dither transparency is allowed for camera occlusion, not as an always-on checkerboard artifact.
- Use a single art-direction `ScriptableObject` palette profile with day/night, Tether/Roots and location variants; transitions must be smooth.

**Illustrative URP HLSL lighting core** (integrate into a full compatible shader, do not paste as a standalone shader):

```hlsl
// Shared painterly diffuse. Validate color-space and shadow attenuation.
// Expected includes: Core.hlsl + Lighting.hlsl from URP.
half3 WB_ToonLighting(
    half3 baseColor, half3 normalWS, half3 positionWS,
    half3 ambientColor, half3 shadowTint, half3 lightTint)
{
    Light sun = GetMainLight(TransformWorldToShadowCoord(positionWS));
    half ndl = saturate(dot(normalize(normalWS), normalize(sun.direction)));
    half intensity = ndl * sun.distanceAttenuation * sun.shadowAttenuation;
    // Three stable bands with soft painterly transitions.
    half mid = smoothstep(0.18h, 0.28h, intensity);
    half high = smoothstep(0.57h, 0.69h, intensity);
    half3 shade = lerp(shadowTint, half3(0.74h, 0.77h, 0.73h), mid);
    shade = lerp(shade, lightTint * sun.color, high);
    return baseColor * (shade + ambientColor);
}
```

This snippet expresses the **lighting intent**. The actual shader must correctly implement additional lights, GI/lightmaps, fog, camera depth, normal maps, transparency/alpha clip and appropriate URP passes as needed. Verify shader compilation in the exact installed URP version. Avoid dynamic material instantiation in `Update`; use shared materials and MaterialPropertyBlocks.

## 5.3 Controlled painterly post-processing

Existing `Assets/Shaders/WishboundPainterly.shader` and `PainterlyLook.cs` can be improved, but the **current danger is blur**. The target is **painted texture and shape preservation**, not a smeared Kuwahara filter over everything.

## 6.1 Fire art specification

A fire is a **designed shape**. At the village hearth, render three or four interleaved 2D flame layers, each with:

- **Embers:** a few tiny angular sparks with upward drift, not 100 fuzzy dots.
- **Smoke:** separate hand-painted 2D wisps, cool desaturated gray/blue, soft but shaped, slower than flame.
- **Heat:** subtle distortion only near the top of the flame and only if supported cheaply.
- **Lighting:** one or two pooled warm lights with gentle flicker (e.g., 2–6 Hz modulated), a contact glow on logs/stone, and shadow variation. **The sprite itself provides most of the fire's beauty.**

## 6.2 Build the original flame flipbook

Preferred: draw 16–32 frames in a 2D editor or generate base contours via Python vector/Bezier curves and **refine their shapes**. The following reproducible Pillow sketch creates an original, anti-aliased, multilayer flame atlas **with pointed silhouettes** rather than fuzzy motes. It is a **base generator**; refine the animation curves, shapes, color bands and timing for final art. Generate separate outer/mid/core atlases with different seeds and frame offsets.

```python
# Example: tools/generate_flame_atlas.py — run in a Python env with Pillow.
# Original procedural 2D shapes; refine with hand-painted edge masks.
from PIL import Image, ImageDraw
import math, random

CELL = 128
COLS = ROWS = 4
SS = 3
atlas = Image.new('RGBA', (COLS * CELL, ROWS * CELL), (0, 0, 0, 0))

def tongue(cx, bottom, width, height, bend, wobble, seed):
    # A tapered, asymmetric flame tongue made from two shaped contours.
    points_left, points_right = [], []
    for i in range(17):
        t = i / 16.0
        y = bottom - height * t
        shift = bend * t * t + math.sin(t * 7 + wobble) * 3 * t
        w = width * ((1 - t) ** 0.78) * (0.88 + .10 * math.sin(t * 8 + seed))
        points_left.append((cx + shift - w, y))
        points_right.append((cx + shift + w * .80, y))
    return points_left + list(reversed(points_right))

for frame in range(COLS * ROWS):
    r = random.Random(900 + frame // 2)
    phase = 2 * math.pi * frame / (COLS * ROWS)
    tile = Image.new('RGBA', (CELL * SS, CELL * SS), (0, 0, 0, 0))
    d = ImageDraw.Draw(tile)
    def scaled(points): return [(round(x * SS), round(y * SS)) for x, y in points]
    # Several independently curling silhouettes, stable base near y=120.
    for i, (cx, h, w) in enumerate([(44, 82, 20), (67, 109, 25), (87, 69, 17)]):
        sway = math.sin(phase + i * 1.7) * 9
        shape = tongue(cx, 121, w, h + 5*math.sin(phase*2+i), sway, phase+i, i)
        d.polygon(scaled(shape), fill=(237, 79, 35, 235))
    for i, (cx, h, w) in enumerate([(53, 67, 15), (72, 88, 17)]):
        sway = math.sin(phase + i * 1.4 + .8) * 7
        d.polygon(scaled(tongue(cx, 121, w, h, sway, phase, i)),
                  fill=(255, 166, 54, 245))
    d.polygon(scaled(tongue(67, 121, 10, 58 + 4*math.sin(phase),
                            math.sin(phase+.5)*4, phase, 4)),
              fill=(255, 236, 159, 255))
    tile = tile.resize((CELL, CELL), Image.Resampling.LANCZOS)
    atlas.alpha_composite(tile, ((frame % COLS) * CELL, (frame // COLS) * CELL))

atlas.save('WB_Fire_FlameAtlas_4x4.png')
```

**Important:** this script is a generative starting point, not an automatic guarantee of AAA-quality animation. Refine the tongue curvature, timing, breakup and hand-painted color edges; preview the loop at actual in-game size. The flames must have **recognizable organic shape evolution**, not identical sliding polygons.

```hlsl
// Core UV selection, not a complete shader.
float2 WB_FlipbookUV(float2 uv, float frame, float columns, float rows)
{
    float total = columns * rows;
    frame = fmod(floor(max(frame, 0.0)), total);
    float2 cell = float2(fmod(frame, columns), floor(frame / columns));
    float2 padding = float2(0.012, 0.012); // tune for actual atlas padding
    float2 local = lerp(padding, 1.0 - padding, uv);
    return (cell + local) / float2(columns, rows);
}
```

```csharp
public sealed class WispSpringMotion
{

    {

        Vector2 target = menuAnchor.HasValue

            : pointer;

        Position = Vector2.SmoothDamp(Position, target, ref velocity,
                                     smoothTime: 0.075f,
```

```csharp
// approximate arc-length lookup for constant-feeling acceleration.
static Vector2 CubicBezier(Vector2 p0, Vector2 p1,
                           Vector2 p2, Vector2 p3, float t)
{
    float u = 1f - Mathf.Clamp01(t);
    return u*u*u*p0 + 3f*u*u*t*p1 + 3f*u*t*t*p2 + t*t*t*p3;
}

static Vector2 CubicTangent(Vector2 p0, Vector2 p1,
                            Vector2 p2, Vector2 p3, float t)
{
    float u = 1f - Mathf.Clamp01(t);
    return 3f*u*u*(p1-p0) + 6f*u*t*(p2-p1) + 3f*t*t*(p3-p2);
}

// Start: actual rendered Wisp position.
// P1: current motion direction * short lead + gentle inward hesitation.
// P2: authored high arc with clearance from UI.
// End: > 10% beyond the viewport bounds, with tangent pointing outward.
// Bank/squash/stretch from tangent and curvature, not random rotations.
```

3. Follows a smoothed spline 1–2 m above ground, with obstacle avoidance and minimum corner radius; wisp may cross small gaps safely but should not mislead the player into an impossible route.
4. Replans on **objective change**, stuck state or moving obstruction, not every frame. Maintain last valid route if a path query fails; fall back to a visible safe waypoint and log a warning.
5. Chooses a visible **staging point** 1–3 m before an interactable, not the exact object's center or inside a wall.
6. Adjusts guide intensity to player behavior: attentive player gets minimal guidance; lost player gets stronger but tasteful cues.
7. Never competes with menu Wisp. World guide is suppressed when UI owns presentation.

## 10.2 Proactive utility AI with memory

Use a hybrid **utility AI + behavior-tree/state-machine execution**:

- **Perception:** detect nearby player, Child, other settlers, resources, weather, workstations, hazards, story events and current time; use throttled queries and explicit events.
- **Needs:** hunger, fatigue, warmth, social need, curiosity, task satisfaction. Needs evolve gradually and influence choices; avoid everyone sleeping or eating during the demo.
- **Job opportunities:** workboard with claim/reservation tokens, priority, required resources, travel cost, urgency and completion effects. Prevent two villagers from using the same tool/workstation unless intentionally cooperative.
- **Personal memory:** stable ID, temperament, familiarity with Star, relationships, last completed job, preferred work area, previous interruption, task outcomes. **Do not use `GetInstanceID()` or position string as persistent identity.**
- **Goal selection:** score available actions using weighted utility; add hysteresis so NPCs don't change jobs every second. Evaluate at ~2–4 Hz for nearby hero NPCs, ~0.2–1 Hz for distant simulation. Animations still update at render rate near camera.
- **Action execution:** move → align to workstation → equip tool → play anticipation → perform with animation event → commit world state → react → cleanly release reservations. World changes must occur on the correct animation contact frame, not instantly when job starts.
- **Interrupts:** story event > safety hazard > urgent job > current work > social idle. On interruption, park tools safely, release reservations if appropriate, retain task progress and resume later.
- **Recovery:** path fails → choose alternate waypoint / safe warp only off camera / abandon and requeue job with cooldown. Never freeze, jitter, spin or repeatedly walk into a wall.

**Example utility scoring** (illustrative; integrate with existing job board):

```csharp
public struct SettlerContext
{
    public float Hunger, Fatigue, Loneliness, Familiarity;
    public bool StoryNeedsHelp;
    public float DistanceToJob;
    public float WorkstationAvailability; // 0..1
}

public static class SettlerUtility
{
    public static float ScoreGardenHelp(in SettlerContext c)
    {
        float roleFit = 0.85f;
        float urgency = c.StoryNeedsHelp ? 1.0f : 0.35f;
        float travel = Mathf.Exp(-c.DistanceToJob / 18f);
        float availability = Mathf.Clamp01(c.WorkstationAvailability);
        float needsPenalty = 0.45f*c.Hunger + 0.25f*c.Fatigue;
        return roleFit * urgency * travel * availability - needsPenalty;
    }
}

// Choose max-utility action only if it exceeds current action by a
// hysteresis margin (e.g., 0.15) or a high-priority story event interrupts.
// Commit resource/quest state at animation events, not at decision time.
```

**Stable identity:** generate serialized GUID per settler prefab/instance at authoring time; keep a migration path for existing saves. Store memory in a versioned save structure with schema validation. Never write dozens of PlayerPrefs keys every frame.

### Quest transition contract

Every quest follows these states:

```text
Inactive → Preparing → Active → Resolving → Completed
                               ↘ Recovery ↗
```

- `Preparing`: ensure location/prefab loaded, NPC actor reserved, guide anchor available, cinematic camera safe.
- `Active`: player control; objectives available; contextual prompts and wisp guidance.
- `Resolving`: interaction locked against duplicate input; completion animation/VFX/NPC reaction plays.
- `Completed`: flags and checkpoint saved once; guide/music/camera updated; next quest begins.
- `Recovery`: player fell, got stuck, NPC missing, puzzle timed out or prefab not loaded; reset only the necessary interaction and recover without breaking story.

**Example exactly-once completion:**

```csharp
using System;
using UnityEngine;
public sealed class QuestCompletionGate : MonoBehaviour
{
    [SerializeField] string questId;
    public bool Completed { get; private set; }
    public event Action<string> CompletedOnce;

    public bool TryComplete()
    {
        if (Completed) return false;
        Completed = true;
        CompletedOnce?.Invoke(questId);
        return true;
    }

    public void Restore(bool completed) => Completed = completed;
}
```

Replace hardcoded magic booleans and route-specific `if` chains where appropriate. Use typed events or stable string IDs with validation; do not create a fragile web of unrelated static flags. Support new run, checkpoint restore and branch replay.

## 11.2 Interaction feel: every action has feedback

For every important player interaction, implement a consistent feedback stack:

1. **Pre-focus:** object silhouette/edge responds subtly as player approaches; wisp settles nearby.
2. **Affordance:** prompt appears at a readable anchor with correct E/F/C glyph and gamepad equivalent; never behind geometry.
3. **Anticipation:** a short charge, reach or movement cue, with audio leading the action.
4. **Commit:** interaction animation and world state change occur at an explicit event frame.
5. **Response:** material, VFX, sound, NPC and camera react together.
6. **Resolution:** prompt disappears, guide transfers, progression saves and next landmark becomes visible.

For held inputs, display a restrained **diegetic progress ring** around the Star/interaction target, with gentle color buildup. No giant generic progress bar unless accessibility mode enables one. Tune hold durations to be meaningful (0.6–1.5s typical; final decision up to 2s), not an artificial way to pad the five minutes.

**Player collision/traversal:** keep forgiving landing zones, gentle ledge correction, robust camera clearance, smooth acceleration, consistent jump/float control and non-punitive failure recovery. No precision platforming that can destroy a live presentation.

## 11.3 Save and checkpoint requirements

- Use a **versioned demo save structure** containing: schema version, run ID, route, phase, quest ID, objective states, Star radiance, Child relationship state, player/Child safe positions, key environment states, relevant NPC story-event states and timing metadata.
- Checkpoint at: post-awakening, post-Child, post-choice, after each quest, before finale.

- Do not save while world state is halfway through a fracture/hand-off unless the restore code can reconstruct it deterministically.
- No persistent state pollution between playtests. Provide editor debug menu for `Reset Demo`, `Jump To Choice`, `Start Tether`, `Start Roots`, `Start Finale`, and `Validate All` — **debug-only**, excluded or hidden in release.

## 11.4 Cutscene and camera authority

The project contains multiple camera controllers (`CutsceneDirector`, `IsoCameraRig`, `CameraDirector`, `IslandThirdPersonController`, Cinemachine). Establish a single camera arbitration system:

- `Gameplay`, `Dialogue`, `TaskReveal`, `Cinematic`, `Menu` camera contexts with explicit priority.
- When a cinematic takes control, gameplay look input is disabled but UI pause/skip behavior remains deliberate. On exit, restore the exact previous camera mode, cursor state, time scale and player control.
- Use Cinemachine blends and authored camera targets, safe FOV ranges, collision avoidance, horizon composition and consistent depth of field. Avoid gratuitous FOV punches and shaky camera during intimate scenes.
- Each major quest gets **one 2–4-second reward camera moment** (optional to skip), not multiple 10-second cutscenes.
- During dialogue, Child's face and Star remain visible; the camera should never cut through foliage or the back of a settler's head.
- The final shots should be composed for both 16:9 projected displays and common desktop aspect ratios.

### Skip and pause semantics

- `Esc` during normal gameplay opens pause; `Esc` during cinematic either offers pause or performs a clearly indicated **skip**, but never commits the Tether/Roots choice.
- Skip must **apply all necessary world-state changes** and move to the next valid phase without orphaning a camera, actor, audio source or particle emitter.
- Pause freezes world animation, quest timers and physics; menu wisp/focus/animation continues using unscaled time.
- QUIT while paused must not resume world time, break the title hold, or leave AudioListener volume muted on next run.
